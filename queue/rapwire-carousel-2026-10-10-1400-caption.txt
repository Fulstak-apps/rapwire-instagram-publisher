1) Summer Walker (@summerwalker) is facing a new legal fight with Interscope Records. The label says she still owes two studio albums and an EP and is seeking damages that could top $50 million after Walker invoked California’s seven-year rule to challenge the deal. Walker’s attorney says she will vigorously defend her position, so the dispute could become a major test of how long recording contracts can bind an artist.

2) Cole Bennett says his Chief Keef documentary should arrive in 2027. The Lyrical Lemonade founder says he and Keef are taking time to tell the Chicago drill icon’s story the right way, with Kenya Barris’ Khalabo Ink Society attached to the project. The update matters because an earlier Apple Music documentary about Keef never materialized, making this the clearest release window yet.

3) Tobe Nwigwe (@tobenwigwe) released The Bridge, a 12-track album built around his Houston upbringing and Nigerian roots. Busta Rhymes opens the record on “CHINEKE,” while Sia, Scarface, Paul Wall, Labrinth and Fat Nwigwe are among the other guests. The project turns Nwigwe’s cross-cultural identity into the organizing idea of a full album instead of a single crossover moment.

4) Jay-Z (@jayz) addressed Ye’s old “Big Brother” complaint about Chris Martin in the finale of Jay-Z in 8. He said Martin was already his friend, that he introduced the two artists, and that no artist owns another person’s collaborations. The answer reframes a famous line from the 2007 song as a disagreement about entitlement, friendship and creative freedom.

5) Usher (@usher) and Chris Brown (@chrisbrownofficial) canceled four R&B Tour dates in Baltimore, Cincinnati, Columbia and Indianapolis. The promoter cited the health and well-being of the touring party as Brown faces an October 26 UK sentencing hearing tied to a 2023 nightclub brawl; the tour is scheduled to resume with five Atlanta shows on November 5. The cancellations leave a blockbuster stadium run moving forward, but under a sharper legal spotlight.
